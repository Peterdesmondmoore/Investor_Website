Current View: The institutional outlook for copper has undergone a significant upward re-rating in the first half of 2026. Major banks and research houses have moved away from the cautious sub-$11,000 estimates seen in late 2025, coalescing around a 2026 annual average that typically exceeds $12,000 per tonne. This shift reflects a deepening conviction in structural supply deficits and the accelerating demand requirements of energy transition and AI infrastructure.

Recent Forecasts: Predictions issued in April 2026 show a tightening consensus, with Goldman Sachs ($12,650), ING ($12,500), and BMO ($12,125) all projecting averages in a similar range. UBS Wealth Management offers the most bullish outlier with a year-end target of $14,500 per tonne, while the World Bank maintains a more conservative but still elevated baseline of $12,000. These figures suggest that the institutional "floor" for copper has shifted higher by approximately $2,000 over the last six months.

Historical Context: The current target distribution represents a clear upward migration from late 2025, when institutions such as the World Bank and DBS Bank held targets in the $9,800 to $9,900 range. The recent median forecast of $12,511 represents an increase of more than $500 over the prior median of $11,975. Older forecasts are now primarily useful as a marker of how significantly supply disruptions and trade-policy uncertainties have forced a repricing of the metal.

Market Drivers: The prevailing upside bias is supported by several recurring themes: acute mine supply disruptions, widening refined copper deficits, and strategic stockpiling. While Goldman Sachs notes that record highs may eventually fade toward $11,000 as surpluses return in later periods, the near-term consensus is driven by the "HALO" trades of defense, AI data centers, and grid modernization, which are viewed as less price-sensitive than traditional industrial demand.

Comparability and Conviction: While the directional consensus is strong, the comparability of the data is occasionally limited by different reporting styles. The dataset includes a mix of annual averages (ING, Goldman), year-end point targets (UBS), and survey averages (Reuters, CME). Survey averages currently sit near $12,000, which aligns closely with individual bank base cases, indicating that the current bullish sentiment is broad-based across the analyst community rather than driven by a few isolated voices.

Outlook Summary: The copper market is currently characterized by high conviction in elevated prices through 2026. The outlook has shifted from "mixed" in late 2025 to "decidedly bullish" in mid-2026. With the International Copper Study Group and other industry bodies confirming a transition from surplus to deficit, the institutional distribution suggests that the primary risk to these targets remains on the upside, specifically regarding the timing of supply normalization and the impact of global trade tariffs.
