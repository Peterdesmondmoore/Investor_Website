US Growth and Business Cycle
Synthesises key growth-related indicators (capex, production, durables, housing, yield-curve and monetary signals) into a cohesive assessment of the current US economic expansion, slowdown or contraction.

Regime Assessment:

  • Regime: Transitional / Fragile Balance
  • Regime Confidence Index: Medium Confidence
  • Status: Fragile

Why This Regime:

  • High-weight business investment momentum remains in an Expansion phase (1), but this is isolated from cooling real-sector activity.
  • Interest-rate-sensitive indicators have inflected, with the Housing Lead signal transitioning from Expansion to Neutral (2).
  • Dynamic weighting prioritizes the High-Confidence Neutral readings in housing and the term structure (3) over the stagnant Industrial Production (4).
  • Industrial Production (4) was downweighted due to internal conflict and low interpretation confidence.
  • Durable Goods ex-Transportation (5) was downweighted due to critical missing data.

Alignment & Tensions:

  • The Housing Lead (2) and Industrial Production (4) reinforce a message of cyclical cooling and stagnation.
  • The term structure signals (3)(6) align on a transition toward steepening, though they currently reside in Neutral or Bearish-trending states.
  • Tension exists between robust core Capex growth (1) and the accommodative neutral rate gap (7) versus broader industrial weakness.
  • These tensions do not overturn the regime call as the divergence in sector momentum is characteristic of a transitional phase.

Scenario Balance:

  • Dominant Scenario: Neutral consolidation as policy lags offset core business demand.
  • Primary Upside Risk: Re-acceleration of core orders >5% YoY (5).
  • Primary Downside Risk: Residential sector contraction triggered by persistent mortgage rate headwinds (2).

What Would Change the Regime:

  • The Capex_Intent score falling below the 0.75 expansion threshold (1).
  • The Yield Curve Composite Z-score falling below 0.50 for two consecutive months (3).
