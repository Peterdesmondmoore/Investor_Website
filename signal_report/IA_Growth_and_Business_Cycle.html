US Growth and Business Cycle
Synthesises key growth-related indicators (capex, production, durables, housing, yield-curve and monetary signals) into a cohesive assessment of the current US economic expansion, slowdown or contraction.

Regime Assessment:

  • Regime: Expansionary / Risk-Positive
  • Regime Confidence Index: High Confidence
  • Status: Stable

Why This Regime:

  • Dominant drivers include Capex Intent (1), which shows High Conviction in a persistent Expansion phase, and the Yield Curve Slope (2), which has transitioned into a Steepening regime.
  • Dynamic weighting prioritized High-weight cyclical signals over tactical or data-deficient ones. The US Treasury Yield Curve (3) further supports this via its move above trend.
  • Durable Goods ex-Transportation (4) was downweighted due to significant data gaps and low interpretation confidence. The Housing Lead Signal (5) was moderated due to high internal conflict between permits and starts.

Alignment & Tensions:

  • Real-sector investment momentum (1) reinforces the financial-sector steepening seen in the term structure (2)(3). Monetary conditions remain supportive, with the neutral rate proxy showing an accommodative stance gap (6).
  • Tensions exist in the housing sector (5), where rising mortgage rates have triggered a shift to Neutral, and in Industrial Production (7), which remains in a moderate Neutral state.
  • These tensions do not overturn the regime call as the highest-weighted cyclical indicators—specifically core capital expenditure and yield curve dynamics—remain aligned in expansionary territory.

Scenario Balance:

  • Dominant Scenario: Sustained expansion driven by business investment and a normalizing yield environment.
  • Primary Upside Risk: Acceleration in industrial output (INDPRO > 2%) triggering a pro-cyclical surge (7).
  • Primary Downside Risk: Monetary restrictiveness shocks where the stance gap narrows significantly above -0.50 (6).

What Would Change the Regime:

  • A Capex_Intent print falling below 0.75, indicating a loss of core investment momentum (1).
  • The Yield Curve Composite falling below 0.50 for two consecutive months, signaling a return to a Flat/Neutral regime (2).
