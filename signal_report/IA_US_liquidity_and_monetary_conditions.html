US Liquidity and Monetary Conditions
Synthesises Federal Reserve liquidity measures, money supply, real-rate signals and SOFR positioning to assess the current US monetary and liquidity regime.

Regime Assessment:

  • Regime: Transitional / Fragile Balance
  • Regime Confidence Index: Medium Confidence
  • The regime is fragile.

Why This Regime:

  • Restrictive real interest rates serve as the primary high-weight structural anchor (1).
  • Stabilizing liquidity aggregates offset restrictive yields, maintaining a neutral macro floor (2).
  • M2 Money Supply was downweighted due to internal threshold conflicts and mixed confidence (3).
  • Positioning signals were downweighted as divergent biases prevent a clear directional consensus (4)(5).

Alignment & Tensions:

  • Monetary aggregates reinforce each other, showing stabilizing momentum in Fed assets and M2 (2)(3).
  • Tensions exist between restrictive real yields and the emerging long bias in five-year swaps (1)(5).
  • Tactical short flow in SOFR-3M conflicts with cyclical stabilization trends (4).
  • Tensions do not overturn the regime because high-weight real rates remain firmly above trend (1).

Scenario Balance:

  • Dominant scenario: Range-bound stability as monetary aggregates and balance sheet growth normalize near zero.
  • Primary upside risk: Shift to Expansionary liquidity if Federal Reserve asset growth accelerates significantly (2).
  • Primary downside risk: Accelerated rise in real borrowing costs further tightening financial conditions (1).

What Would Change the Regime:

  • Liquidity Composite exceeding 0.75 would confirm an Expansionary regime shift (2).
  • Real yield mean-reversion to within 0.05% of the six-month trend would remove the bearish anchor (1).
