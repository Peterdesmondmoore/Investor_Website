Fed Projections Synthesiser Summary

Inference of the Federal Reserve-s internally prepared future economic state, based exclusively on Fed-authored projections and communications.

1. Fed-Implied Growth Outlook

The Federal Reserve is preparing for a state of resilient but moderating growth, with real GDP projected at 2.4 percent for 2026 before converging toward a 2.0 percent longer-run trend by 2028 (1). While current indicators suggest expansion at a "solid pace" (2), the internal preparedness scenario accounts for a transition from "slight to modest" regional growth toward a more cautious posture as firms adopt a "wait-and-see" approach to capital investment (3). Confidence in this trajectory is tempered by a significant majority of policymakers now viewing the uncertainty attached to growth projections as "higher" than historical norms (1).

2. Fed-Implied Inflation Trajectory

The Fed is preparing for a "sticky" inflation environment where the return to the 2 percent objective is delayed by supply-side shocks. Projections for PCE inflation in 2026 have been revised upward to 2.7 percent, with core inflation also expected to remain at 2.7 percent (1). Communications explicitly highlight that inflation remains "elevated," exacerbated by recent increases in global energy prices (2). This trajectory is highly sensitive to the Middle East conflict, which has already driven "sharp" increases in energy and fuel costs across all Federal Reserve Districts (3). The Fed does not expect to reach its 2 percent target until 2028 (1).

3. Fed-Implied Labour Market Trajectory

The Federal Reserve appears to be preparing for a gradual softening of the labour market, projecting an unemployment rate of 4.4 percent through 2026—up from previous baseline assessments (1). While current "job gains have remained low" and the market is described as "stable" with low turnover, the Fed is monitoring a shift where firms are increasingly utilizing AI-driven productivity to delay or reduce permanent hiring (3). The primary concern is no longer overheating, but rather the potential for "policy risks" to shift as the balance between inflation and employment becomes more symmetric (4).

4. Dominant Risk Narrative

The dominant narrative governing Fed preparation is the "high level of uncertainty" stemming from geopolitical developments in the Middle East (2). This is framed as a supply-side risk that weights inflation to the upside while simultaneously posing downside risks to growth (1). The Fed is specifically preparing for "One Transitory Shock After Another," which complicates the timing of policy adjustments and necessitates a "careful assessment" of whether external shocks are impeding the return to price stability (2).

5. Evidence Map (Concise)

  • Key SEP signals: Upward revisions to 2026 PCE and Core PCE inflation (to 2.7%); 2026 GDP revised up to 2.4%; dot plot median at 3.4% for year-end 2026 (1).
  • Key Beige Book themes: "Wait-and-see posture" regarding hiring and investment; "sharp" energy price increases; consumer financial strain among lower-income households (3).
  • Key language or framing: "Inflation is elevated"; "High level of uncertainty" due to Middle East; dissent over "easing bias" indicates internal divergence on the readiness to cut rates (2).

6. Missing or Incomplete Inputs

The full text of the Minutes of the Federal Open Market Committee, April 28-29, 2026 was not provided for this analysis, although its publication was detected via RSS (5). This document is critical for validating the internal debate regarding the "easing bias" and the specific threshold for "additional adjustments." Additionally, while the Policy Risks Have Changed speech (4) was processed, the absence of the Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2025 report—which was identified as processed in the library but not explicitly provided in the core analysis set—may limit the synthesis of consumer-side risks. These documents are typically available via the Federal Reserve's primary RSS feeds.
