US Credit Conditions
Integrates credit spreads, systemic-stress indicators, and private-credit growth signals to assess the prevailing US credit-conditions regime.

Regime Assessment:

  • Regime: Transitional / Fragile Balance
  • Regime Confidence Index: Low Confidence
  • Stability: Fragile

Why This Regime:

  • The assessment is driven by a conflict between expansionary credit volume and neutral systemic stress indicators (5). While credit spreads remain at levels indicating abundant availability (1), aggregate financial stress has only recently stabilized into a neutral zone (5).
  • Dynamic weighting downweighted the Credit Spreads (1) and Financial Stress Composite (2) signals due to Low Interpretation Confidence and significant missing data components in the latest reporting periods.
  • The "Transitional" label is selected as the most conservative interpretation of a recent shift from high systemic stress toward a neutral environment, shadowed by internal conflicts in data reporting (5)(3).

Alignment & Tensions:

  • Alignment exists between tactical volatility measures and cyclical credit spreads, both of which suggest a return to normal functioning (5)(3).
  • Tension exists where Private Credit Impulse shows cyclical acceleration (4), yet systemic stress composites remain pinned at neutral, lacking corroborating momentum in credit spreads (2).
  • These tensions do not overturn the regime call because the acceleration in credit impulse relies on incomplete component data (4).

Scenario Balance:

  • Dominant Scenario: Range-bound stability as credit demand normalizes.
  • Primary Upside Risk: Sustained credit acceleration (Impulse > 0.75) if business and consumer loans align with bank credit growth (4).
  • Primary Downside Risk: Sudden liquidity shock triggering a regime flip to Bearish/Tightening via a VIX-led spike or spread widening (3)(5).

What Would Change the Regime:

  • A rise in the Financial Stress Index z-score above +0.5 would confirm a shift to Defensive/Risk-Negative (5).
  • An increase in HY spreads exceeding 50 bps within three months would invalidate the current balance (1).
  • Continued Private Credit Impulse readings above 0.75 for two months alongside confirmed loan data would trigger an Expansionary regime (4).
