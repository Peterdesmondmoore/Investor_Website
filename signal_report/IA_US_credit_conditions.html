US Credit Conditions
Integrates credit spreads, systemic-stress indicators, and private-credit growth signals to assess the prevailing US credit-conditions regime.

Regime Assessment:

  • Regime Label: Expansionary / Risk-Positive
  • Regime Confidence Index: Medium Confidence
  • The regime is stable.

Why This Regime:

  • The primary driver is the accelerating private sector credit growth confirmed over two months (4).
  • Bank credit acceleration provides a high-weight cyclical impulse for mid-cycle expansion (4).
  • Corporate funding costs remain exceptionally low, with compressed high-yield spreads of 2.79% (1).
  • The Credit Spreads signal was downweighted due to missing composite z-scores and mixed interpretation confidence (1).
  • Private Credit Impulse was downweighted slightly for relying on bank credit while lacking specific business loan data (4).

Alignment & Tensions:

  • Credit expansion and spread levels align to support a risk-on environment (4)(1).
  • Tensions exist as financial stress indices remain in neutral territory despite the expansionary credit impulse (2)(5).
  • Current neutral readings represent a stabilizing recovery from early-year volatility rather than active stress (3)(5).
  • Tensions do not overturn the regime because credit creation typically leads broader financial stress shifts (4).

Scenario Balance:

  • Dominant scenario: Continued trend expansion supported by abundant liquidity and low funding costs (1).
  • Primary upside risk: Re-acceleration of business loan demand as corporate capital expenditure cycles turn (4).
  • Primary downside risk: Sudden liquidity shocks causing bid-ask spread inflation and a regime flip (1).

What Would Change the Regime:

  • High-yield spreads widening by more than 50 basis points over a three-month window (1).
  • A move in the composite credit conditions index above the 0.75 tightening threshold (3).
  • A reversal in credit momentum pulling the impulse signal back into the neutral zone (4).

Methodology Applied:

  • Rising composite values indicate a transition toward risk-off or tightening regimes, while falling values indicate accommodative shifts. (1)
  • The model uses standardized averages of the St. Louis Fed Financial Stress Index (STLFSI4), High-Yield Spreads (HY OAS), and the 10Y-2Y yield curve slope. (1)

Key Dynamics:

Methodology Applied:

  • A Bullish regime is defined by STLFSI4 < 0 AND a z-score < -0.5, implying loose financial conditions (1).
  • A Bearish regime occurs if STLFSI4 > 0 OR a z-score > +0.5, signaling elevated systemic stress (1).
  • The Neutral regime represents normal market functioning or a transitional state between risk extremes (1).
  • Dataset: St. Louis Fed Financial Stress Index (STLFSI4), latest observation 2026-05-01 (1).

Key Dynamics:

Scenario Balance:
